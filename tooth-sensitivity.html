Why are my teeth sensitive?

Tooth sensitivity is a short, sharp feeling in a tooth, most often triggered by cold, hot, sweet or sour foods and drinks, cold air, or brushing. It usually happens when the softer layer under the enamel, called dentin, becomes exposed. Common causes include:

  • Gum recession that exposes the root surface
  • Enamel worn down by acidic foods and drinks
  • Brushing too hard or with a hard toothbrush
  • Clenching or grinding the teeth
  • Recent whitening or dental treatment
  • A cavity, a cracked tooth or a worn filling

How we can help

We begin with an assessment to find which teeth are sensitive and what is likely causing it. Depending on what we find, your visit may include:

  • A professional desensitizing treatment applied to the sensitive areas, as a varnish, gel or other topical treatment
  • Fluoride treatment to help strengthen the tooth surface
  • Advice on brushing technique, toothbrush choice and toothpaste for sensitive teeth
  • Guidance on foods and drinks that can wear enamel
  • Referral to a dentist if the sensitivity points to a cavity, a crack or another problem that needs their care

How much a desensitizing treatment helps, and how long it lasts, varies from person to person. Some people need more than one application.

Sensitive during cleanings?

If cleanings have been uncomfortable for you in the past, please tell us when you book. We can go at your pace and take breaks. We can also use desensitizing materials during the teeth cleaning itself to help reduce sensitivity while we work, and apply a desensitizing treatment before or after it.

When to see a dentist

Sensitivity that stays in one tooth, lasts a long time after the trigger is gone, wakes you at night, or comes with swelling can be a sign of a problem that a hygienist cannot treat. If we see signs of this at your assessment, we will explain what we found and refer you to a dentist.

Tooth sensitivity questions

What causes sensitive teeth?

Most often it is exposed dentin, the softer layer under the enamel, from gum recession, enamel wear, hard brushing or grinding. Sensitivity can also come from a cavity or a cracked tooth, which is why an assessment comes first.

How does a desensitizing treatment work?

A professional desensitizing agent is applied to the sensitive areas of the tooth. It helps cover or block the tiny channels in the dentin that carry sensations to the nerve. The application takes a few minutes.

Will one treatment be enough?

It varies from person to person. Some people notice a difference after one application, and others need repeat applications along with changes to home care. We'll review how you are doing at your next visit.

Can sensitive teeth still be cleaned?

Yes. Let us know about your sensitivity when you book. We can adapt the cleaning, take breaks, use desensitizing materials during the cleaning to help reduce sensitivity, and apply a desensitizing treatment before or after it.

Can I have my teeth whitened if they are sensitive?

Possibly. Sensitivity is reviewed at your whitening assessment before any treatment, and we will tell you honestly if whitening is not right for you.

Is treatment for sensitive teeth covered by insurance or CDCP?

Desensitizing treatment is covered by some dental insurance plans and may be covered under the Canadian Dental Care Plan (CDCP). Coverage depends on your plan, so we recommend checking it before your visit, and we are happy to help. Not sure if you're eligible or how to apply? We're happy to help you apply.
